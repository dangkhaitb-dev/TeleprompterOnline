12 Common Teleprompter Mistakes and How to Fix Them

Most teleprompter problems come from the script, the reading position, the scrolling method, or insufficient testing. Identify the visible symptom, change one variable, and record a short test before changing anything else.

Most teleprompter problems come from four areas: the script, the reading position, the scrolling method, or insufficient testing.

Fix the problem by identifying the visible symptom, changing one variable, and recording another short test.

1. The reading area is too wide

Symptom

Your eyes move visibly from left to right.

Likely cause

The script stretches across a large part of the monitor.

Fix

Narrow the text column and move it closer to the camera.

The column should show enough words for a short phrase without requiring the eyes to cross the display.

Retest

Record ten seconds at three widths. Review the video at normal size.

2. The text is too far from the camera

Symptom

You appear to look below or beside the viewer.

Likely cause

The active line is separated from the lens.

Fix

Move the reading area immediately below or beside the camera.

Stanford research on video communication shows that camera position affects visible gaze cues and that viewers can be sensitive to directional misalignment. This supports testing the actual recording rather than choosing one universal distance. Stanford dissertation.

Retest

Record from the center of the screen, below the camera, and slightly beside the camera. Compare the clips.

3. The script sounds like an article

Symptom

Your voice becomes formal, careful, and unlike normal conversation.

Likely cause

The script was written for silent reading.

Fix

Use conversational language, short sentences, and one main idea at a time.

Broadcast-writing guidance recommends writing for the ear and making the message understandable when heard once. University of Arkansas.

Retest

Explain the same point without the script. Compare your natural wording with the draft and rewrite the difference.

4. The sentences are too long

Symptom

You run out of breath, lose emphasis, or restart near the end of sentences.

Likely cause

One sentence contains several ideas and qualifications.

Fix

Split the sentence where the idea changes. Put important qualifications in a separate sentence rather than removing them.

Retest

Read the new version aloud without the teleprompter. If you still need an unnatural breath, split it again.

5. You read one word at a time

Symptom

Every word receives the same emphasis and the rhythm sounds mechanical.

Likely cause

Your eyes and voice move through the script at the same point.

Fix

Absorb short phrases before speaking them. Use line breaks to group meaning.

Example:

The teleprompter should support your pace.
It should not force you
to chase the screen.

Retest

Record the original paragraph and the phrase-grouped version.

6. The fixed speed controls your voice

Symptom

You rush when the text moves ahead or stretch words while waiting for it.

Likely cause

The selected WPM does not match the delivery.

Fix

Start slower. Rehearse the full section and increase the speed gradually.

Use voice-assisted scrolling when your pace changes significantly, or manual control when you improvise.

7. You rely on voice recognition without testing

Symptom

The script does not advance, loses position, or behaves differently during the real recording.

Likely cause

The browser, microphone, language, noise, or spoken wording differs from the rehearsal conditions.

Fix

Test:

  • Microphone permission.
  • Input level.
  • Browser recognition.
  • Background noise.
  • Script language.
  • Long pauses.
  • Repeated and skipped sentences.

Keep fixed-speed or manual control available.

SolPrompter’s Terms state that automated features may mishear, lose position, or scroll at an unsuitable pace. Terms.

8. The font is uncomfortable

Symptom

You lean forward, squint, or lose the next phrase.

Likely cause

The font is too small, too large, or poorly spaced for the viewing distance.

Fix

Increase the font until the face and posture relax. Keep enough text visible to anticipate the next phrase.

Use strong contrast and paragraph spacing.

Retest

Record at the actual distance. Do not choose font size while sitting closer to the screen than you will during the take.

9. You try to reproduce every word perfectly

Symptom

A minor wording change causes a complete restart.

Likely cause

You are treating the script as a memory test.

Fix

Understand the purpose of each section. Allow small wording differences when exact language is not required.

Preserve exact wording for:

  • Legal statements.
  • Safety instructions.
  • Medical information.
  • Financial disclosures.
  • Approved claims.
  • Direct quotations.

Retest

Practice returning to the main idea after replacing or skipping one word.

10. You rehearse with a different setup

Symptom

The delivery looks natural during practice but not during the real take.

Likely cause

The camera, reading distance, screen, font, orientation, or microphone changed.

Fix

Rehearse with the final setup.

Use the same:

  • Device.
  • Camera.
  • Browser.
  • Reading position.
  • Font and margins.
  • Scroll mode.
  • Microphone.
  • Lighting.
  • Recording orientation.

11. You record a take that is too long

Symptom

The beginning is strong, but errors and tension increase later.

Likely cause

The take exceeds the presenter’s comfortable concentration or the script contains no natural break points.

Fix

Divide the recording into logical sections when the format allows it.

Use paragraphs, transitions, or slide changes as edit points.

A long single take may still be necessary for live or continuous presentations. In that case, add rehearsal, an operator, and backup controls.

12. You have no backup control method

Symptom

One recognition, browser, or device problem stops the production.

Likely cause

The workflow depends on a single automated feature.

Fix

Prepare at least one alternative:

  • Fixed-speed scrolling.
  • Manual keyboard control.
  • Touch scrolling.
  • A second device as remote.
  • Printed notes.
  • A backup script file.
  • A physical operator.

For high-stakes production, test the backup before the event.

Frequently asked questions

Why do I look like I am reading?

The text may be too wide, too far from the camera, or too difficult to scan. You may also be reading individual words rather than phrases.

Why does my voice sound robotic?

The script may use written language, the scrolling may control your pace, or you may be focusing on exact reproduction instead of meaning.

How do I stop chasing the teleprompter?

Lower the fixed speed, switch to voice-assisted scrolling after testing it, or use manual control.

Why does voice scrolling lose my place?

Recognition can be affected by browser support, microphone quality, noise, language, pronunciation, and large departures from the script.

Should I record one long take?

Only when the format requires it. Logical sections can reduce pressure and create natural edit points.
