How to Set Up a Teleprompter Without Special Hardware

You can use a browser teleprompter with a laptop, tablet, or phone without buying beam-splitter glass. Place the active reading area near the camera, narrow the text column, choose a readable font, and record a short eye-line test.

Reviewed by the SolPrompter team · Updated September 5, 2026

Open SolPrompter

You can use a browser teleprompter with a laptop, tablet, or phone without buying beam-splitter glass. Position the active reading area near the camera, narrow the text column, choose a readable font, and record a short eye-line test before the real take.

A screen-based setup will not place the words directly over the lens in every case. The objective is to reduce the visible offset enough for your framing and content.

What you need

For a basic browser setup:

  • A laptop, tablet, or phone.
  • A browser.
  • A prepared script.
  • A camera if the device is not recording.
  • A microphone if you need audio or voice-assisted scrolling.
  • A stable stand or surface.
  • A short test recording.

SolPrompter runs in the browser and allows users to paste or upload a script, choose a scroll mode, adjust the reading view, and record on supported devices. Complete product guide.

Laptop webcam setup

A laptop is the simplest starting point.

  • Place the laptop at eye level.
  • Open the teleprompter.
  • Move the active reading area toward the top center.
  • Narrow the text so the eyes do not scan across the screen.
  • Increase the font until you can sit normally.
  • Record ten to thirty seconds.
  • Adjust the reading position based on the recording.

Avoid placing the laptop low on a desk. This encourages the presenter to look downward and can create an unflattering camera angle.

Use a stand or stable stack to raise the device. Keep the screen and webcam close to eye height.

External webcam setup

An external webcam gives you more control over the camera position.

Place the webcam:

  • At the top center of the monitor.
  • Directly above a compact reading window.
  • On a small mount close to the visible text.
  • At eye level rather than high above or below the face.

The webcam does not have to remain attached to the display. A small tripod may let you place it closer to the reading column.

Check that the camera does not cover important words or controls.

Phone setup

A phone can function as:

  • The recording camera and teleprompter.
  • A separate teleprompter beside another camera.
  • A remote control for a teleprompter running on another device.

When the phone is both camera and teleprompter:

  • Identify the front-camera position.
  • Place the active text near that camera.
  • Use a narrow column.
  • Keep only a small number of lines visible.
  • Mount the phone securely.
  • Record in the final orientation: portrait or landscape.
  • Review eye-line and audio.

A phone screen shows less context than a laptop. Use shorter paragraphs and clearer line breaks.

Tablet setup

A tablet provides a larger reading area than a phone while remaining portable.

Use it:

  • Directly below an external camera.
  • Beside a phone that is recording.
  • As the primary teleprompter for a person sitting at a desk.
  • As a remote-control device.

A larger screen can create wider eye movement if the text stretches across the full display. Keep the reading column compact.

Camera with a separate display

For a mirrorless, DSLR, or cinema camera, position a laptop, tablet, or phone as close to the lens as the framing permits.

This setup works best when:

  • The speaker is close to the camera.
  • The shot is not an extreme close-up.
  • The reading column is narrow.
  • The presenter can read the text without turning the head.

When the speaker stands farther away, a physical beam-splitter rig may provide a better optical alignment.

Academic teleconferencing systems have used beam-splitter arrangements to place a camera and reflected visual content near a shared viewing axis. This explains the principle, but it does not mean every rig uses one identical angle or design. USC ICT paper.

Position the text near the lens

Research on video communication shows that camera position affects the appearance of gaze and conversational cues. Viewers can be sensitive to the direction of the visible offset. Stanford research.

Use this practical method:

  • Place the reading area directly below the camera.
  • Record a short clip.
  • Move it slightly left or right if the camera is not centered.
  • Compare the clips at normal viewing size.
  • Keep the position that looks most direct without becoming difficult to read.

Do not rely on a universal distance such as sixty centimeters or two feet. Screen size, lens position, framing, and presenter distance change the perceived result.

Choose font size and reading width

Increase the font until you can:

  • Read without leaning.
  • Keep the face relaxed.
  • See enough context to anticipate the next phrase.
  • Maintain a natural posture.

Narrow the text until the eyes do not travel across most of the display.

The correct combination depends on:

  • Viewing distance.
  • Screen size.
  • Camera position.
  • Vision.
  • Script complexity.
  • Recording framing.

SolPrompter currently provides adjustable font sizes, margins, alignment, and an eye-line guide. Product guide.

Select a scrolling method

Choose among:

Fixed speed

Useful for rehearsed delivery with a consistent pace.

Voice-assisted scrolling

Useful when the pace and pauses change. Performance depends on speech-recognition conditions such as browser support, microphone quality, language, pronunciation, and noise.

Manual scrolling

Useful when the presenter prefers direct control.

Remote control

Useful when the recording device should not be touched or when another person operates the teleprompter.

Test camera, microphone, and performance

Before an important recording, test:

  • Camera permission.
  • Microphone permission.
  • Audio level.
  • Framing.
  • Lighting.
  • Text position.
  • Scroll behavior.
  • Recording resolution.
  • Browser performance.
  • Storage availability.
  • A backup workflow.

Browser features can vary with the device, browser version, hardware, available memory, extensions, and power-saving settings. SolPrompter documents these limitations in its feature notes and Terms.

Change one variable at a time. If you change camera position, text width, font size, and speed together, you will not know which adjustment helped.

When to buy a physical teleprompter

Consider a beam-splitter rig when:

  • The speaker stands far from the camera.
  • The shot is a close-up.
  • Direct optical alignment is important.
  • A professional camera and lens are used.
  • Long scripts must remain easy to read.
  • The same setup is used regularly.
  • A remote operator controls the text.

A browser setup remains useful inside a physical rig when it supports Mirror Mode. SolPrompter includes a horizontal Mirror Mode for reflected teleprompter systems. Product guide.

Common setup mistakes

  • Placing the device below eye level.
  • Using text across the full monitor.
  • Choosing a font that requires leaning.
  • Recording before testing the final orientation.
  • Assuming mobile and desktop behave identically.
  • Depending on voice recognition without a backup.
  • Moving the camera after rehearsal.
  • Using ordinary glass and expecting professional beam-splitter performance.

For diagnosis, read Common Teleprompter Mistakes.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use my phone as a teleprompter and camera?

Yes. Keep the text close to the front camera, use a compact reading area, and review a test clip because the small screen limits context.

Can I use a laptop without beam-splitter glass?

Yes. Position the reading area near the webcam. The words will not be directly over the lens, so eye-line testing remains important.

Where should I put the teleprompter?

Place the active text as close to the lens as practical, then compare short recordings. Do not rely on one universal physical measurement.

What font size should I use?

Use a size that is comfortable at the final reading distance and still shows enough context for the next phrase.

Is a physical teleprompter better?

It can provide better optical alignment for close-ups, long distances, and professional cameras. A browser setup is faster and less expensive for many laptop, phone, and desk workflows.
